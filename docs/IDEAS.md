# Ancient Warfare Reforged: ideas for new additions

Brainstorm from 2026-11-25. Nothing here is decided. Size: S is a few hours, M is a phase-sized piece of work, L is several phases. My top picks are marked ★.

## Settlements that grow and change
- ★ **Settlers arrive (M).** A settlement with high morale, free beds and spare food attracts a new settler every few days, who shows up at the town hall asking to join. Low morale makes people leave. This replaces buying every NPC with a growth loop.
- ★ **Settlement levels (M).** Hamlet, village, town, city, based on population, buildings and wealth. Each level unlocks more job slots, a bigger boundary, new NPC types and new orders.
- **Buildings with a purpose (M).** Recognise rooms by their contents, the way villagers recognise beds: a barracks (beds plus an armour stand), a granary (chests plus hay), a chapel, a tavern. Each counts toward morale and levels and gives a small bonus.
- **Taxes and a treasury (S).** Members pay coins into the hall from their work. The treasury pays upkeep for soldiers and buys from caravans automatically.
- **Laws and policies (M).** Toggles at the hall with trade-offs: rationing (less food, less morale), conscription (workers fight in an alarm), curfew, a market day.

## NPC life
- ★ **Families and children (L).** Couples with homes and high morale have children who grow into workers over a few in-game weeks. Children inherit a surname. Works well with names and the talk screen.
- **Skills and experience (M).** Workers level up in their job: a skilled miner digs faster and finds more ore, a veteran soldier hits harder. The talk screen shows ranks such as "Apprentice Miner Bill".
- **Relationships (M).** Friends and rivals between NPCs. Friends boost each other's morale and fight better side by side. A friend's death hits harder.
- **Traits (S).** One or two per NPC, like lazy, brave, glutton, devout or night owl, that bend morale and work speed. Shown on the talk screen.
- **Holidays and festivals (S).** Occasional days off with a feast at the campfire for a morale boost, paid for with food from storage.

## War and factions
- ★ **NPC faction raids (M).** Hostile NPC factions send war parties against player settlements. Strength scales with settlement level, and you get a warning from scouts or a caravan. Defending well raises reputation with that faction's enemies.
- ★ **NPC faction settlements (L).** NPC factions build and hold their own villages, made with the gates and normal blocks rather than the old template system. They trade, grow and go to war with each other based on their relations.
- **Sieges with objectives (M).** Capture a faction's banner block to take its settlement, or make it pay tribute.
- **Mercenaries (S).** Hire faction soldiers for coins for a few days. Their loyalty depends on pay and morale.
- **Formations and squads (M).** Group soldiers into named squads with a captain. Formations (line, wedge, shield wall) change defence and speed. The baton orders a whole squad at once.
- **Banners and heraldry (S).** A faction banner design from Spectre Factions shown on NPC shields, tabards and ship sails.

## Vehicles and the sea
- **Naval combat improvements (M).** Boarding: grappling hooks pull two ships together so crews fight across. Ramming damage for the war galley. Sinking ships drop part of their cargo as floating crates.
- ★ **Sea trade routes (M).** Trader ships run routes between your ports like the wagon routes, using docks as stops. Faction trade ships visit your docks the way caravans visit by land.
- **More siege engines (M).** Siege tower (troops climb the walls), mantlet (mobile cover), fire-arrow ballista, mangonel. They use the existing vehicle framework.
- **Roads (S).** Path or gravel blocks give carts, wagons and walking NPCs a speed boost. Caravans prefer roads.
- **Horse breeding for war horses (S).** The stable hand breeds horses with better speed and health for cavalry and carts.

## Economy and production
- **Production chains (M).** Sawmill (logs to planks and beams), tannery (leather), smithy (an NPC smith makes tools and weapons from ingots for your soldiers), bakery. Each is a staffed worksite.
- **Supply and demand prices (S).** Market prices drift with stock: plenty makes goods cheap, scarcity makes them dear. Caravans buy where cheap and sell where dear.
- **Contracts (S).** Faction traders post requests ("50 iron for 12 gold coins and reputation") that any player can fill.

## Exploration and quests
- **Faction quests (M).** Faction NPCs give small tasks for reputation: deliver goods, clear a cave of hostiles, escort a caravan.
- **Ruins and camps (M).** Small hand-made world-gen points of interest, like a bandit camp, an abandoned watchtower or a shipwreck, with loot and an NPC faction connection. Just a few handcrafted pieces, not the old template system.
- **A map table (M).** A block that shows your settlements, caravans, ships and known faction lands on a map.

## Quality of life
- ★ **Settlement overview screen (S).** One screen at the hall listing every NPC with job, mood, location and a "what's wrong" flag (hungry, no tool, no bed).
- **Work orders by area (S).** Draw an area with the baton and tell workers "clear this", "fill this", "build a wall along here" from a material chest.
- **JEI/EMI pages (S).** Show worksite outputs, NPC trades and caravan goods in JEI.
- **Jade/WTHIT tooltips (S).** Looking at an NPC shows its name, job, mood and current task without opening a screen.

## Recommended next three
1. **Settlers arrive + settlement levels.** This makes the colony feel alive and gives long-term goals. It builds on the morale, beds and food already done.
2. **NPC faction raids.** This gives a reason to build walls, gates and guards, and puts Spectre Factions relations to work.
3. **Settlement overview screen + Jade tooltips.** These are cheap, and they make everything above easier to understand while playing.
